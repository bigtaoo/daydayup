## A death burst is the size of what died (2026-09-03, client + engine event field, no ENGINE_VERSION bump)

Live report, with a screenshot of a corpse mid-dissolve: *the effect itself reads well, it is
just far too big — make it a circle, with the radius decided by the monster's own body.* An
unusually well-specified report: it names what to keep, what is wrong, and the shape of the fix.

**What the burst actually was, and why "too big" was the smaller half of the finding.**
`ParticleSystem.explosionDebris` had stood since 2026-07-26 (design/01 milestone 4) as one
authored spray for every death: 6-8 pieces, each rolling its own speed (70-160 px/s) and its own
lifetime (260-500 ms), under `gravity: 200`. Multiply that out and the reach is **anywhere from
18 to 80 world px**, drifting downward on the way. Two consequences fall out, and the report is
both of them:

- **Its size had nothing to do with the thing dying.** The basic mob draws a 15 px body radius
  and the boss 30 (`engine/content/enemies.ts`), so the same spray was **up to 5 body radii on
  the mob and 2.7 on the boss** — i.e. a mob's death was the bigger event of the two, which is
  backwards, and no number in the fx knew a body radius existed.
- **Its shape was a plume, not a burst.** With each piece rolling its own speed and its own
  lifetime, where any one of them ended up was independent of every other, and the gravity term
  pulled the whole population off-centre while it travelled. It read as an explosion the corpse
  happened to be standing in.

**The fix is to stop authoring the size and solve it from the body.** Every piece now travels
the SAME distance — `DEBRIS_REACH_R` = 1.6 body radii — over one shared `DEBRIS_LIFE_MS` = 300,
with speed computed as `distance / life` rather than rolled. What the eye follows is one
expanding circle whose final radius is a property of the corpse: 24 px for a mob, 48 for a boss.
The jitter that keeps it from reading mechanical moved to where it cannot change the answer —
the angle, and ±12% of the radius. The piece count is held proportional to the body against a
15 px reference, so a boss's doubled circumference keeps the same angular density (7 pieces
becomes 14) instead of reading as a handful of stray dots.

**The gravity term is gone, and that is the part worth recording.** It is the only
non-radial force in the burst, and at a mob's 24 px reach its 200 px/s² was worth **~9 px of sag
by the end — better than a third of the ring's own radius**, which is exactly the difference
between a circle and a teardrop. `shieldShards` is a ring too and keeps a token `gravity: 70`
for the reason it always did: it is thrown at 130-220 px/s, so the same sag is a small fraction
of that reach. Same term, opposite verdict, because the reach is different — which is why the
comment in each says so rather than either one just carrying a number.

**The centre moved with the size.** The burst was spawned at the event position lifted by a flat
`- 12` px. That is 0.8 body radii on the 15 px mob it was eyeballed on and 0.4 on a boss, so a
boss ring hung around its feet; it is now that same 0.8 fraction of whatever died. Deliberately
an approximation rather than `Actor`'s own `bodyCenterY`: that number is per-SKIN (a rig stands
its art off the ground point inside its body bone, a Graphics placeholder gets `BODY_LIFT_R`),
and reading it would mean resolving a view this reactor cannot see — see the next paragraph.

**The engine had to carry the radius, and this is the one lifecycle event where that is not a
shortcut.** `GameEvent`'s `death` gained `r: Fp`, the dying actor's DRAWN body radius (never one
of the three collision radii, `state/actorRadius.ts`). `events.ts`'s header is explicit that a
render-side read of live state is preferred over a new event field — `bullet_fired` deliberately
omits the melee hit window for exactly that reason, because every client holds the whole
`GameState`. Neither escape hatch exists here:

- `DeathDropsSystem` compacts the dead enemy out of `state.enemies` **on the same tick it pushes
  the event**, so by the time a frame's events are consumed there is no entity left to read.
- `Scene.actorAt` cannot stand in either: the view has already left `Scene.views` for the dying
  list by then, which `render/rigClipLayer.ts` had already documented for its own reasons.

Additive and inert for the sim, so **no `ENGINE_VERSION` bump** — `serializeState` carries no
`events`, which is checkable rather than assumed and was checked. Both producers set it
(`DeathDropsSystem` for enemies, `ReviveSystem` for a player's permanent death).

### What guards it

Fourteen cases across the three layers, twelve of them net new (one pair replaced the old
count-band tests). The useful thing to record is which mistake each one exists for: the geometry
here is all plausible-looking arithmetic, and every claim below was confirmed by a mutant.

- **Sized off the body, measured at BOTH ends of the roster.** A ring measured at one body size
  cannot tell "sized off the body" from "an authored constant that happens to fit this body",
  which is the exact bug being replaced. Mob and boss, same dice, mean radius asserted against
  1.6× each and their ratio against 2.
- **Lands as a circle, under a JITTERED prng rather than a pinned one.** With `Math.random`
  mocked to a constant, "every piece the same distance out" is true by construction even if the
  speed were rolled. One bound — every piece inside 1 ± 0.12 of the reach — kills the rolled
  speed AND the gravity term at once: 9 px of sag at a 24 px reach would throw the down-going
  pieces to 1.4× and the up-going ones to 0.6×.
- **The budget owns the count, not the reach.** The quality tier may make a burst sparser; it may
  not make a mob's death a different size on a weaker machine. `scaled(reach)` reads just as
  reasonably as `scaled(count)` in a diff, and `setBudget`'s own doc comment calls itself a
  multiplier on "every burst count".
- **Debris stays physical, not additive.** The only property of the burst that is not geometry,
  so nothing else here can see it; the neighbouring spawners in the file split on it
  (`muzzleFlame`/`shieldShards` are light, casings and debris are matter).
- **End-to-end through the real engine**, in `EventReactor.test.ts`: a real `buildEnemyActor`
  killed by a real `DeathDropsSystem`, so the event's contents are measured rather than trusted.
  It catches which radius the engine puts on the wire, which field the reactor reads, and the
  fp→px conversion between them — dropping that call would ship a burst ~32× too big, and it is
  the single easiest mistake in the file, since `gx`/`gy` need the same call two lines away.
- **Both producers**, engine-side, each asserted against the actor its own real builder makes
  (`buildEnemyActor` / `createGameState`) rather than against a hand-made fixture, so a roster or
  `PLAYER_BASE` change moves the expectation instead of falsifying the test. Each also asserts
  `radius !== footprintRadius`, so the claim is about WHICH radius and not an accident of two
  numbers being equal today — the substitution that has already gone wrong twice on the engine
  side of this same value (`DoorSystem`, the minion clamp; both fixed at v49).

**Nine mutants run, nine killed, each by exactly the test written for it** and by no other, so
none of the nine duplicates coverage that already existed: gravity restored, reach fixed at the
reference body, speed rolled per piece, count not scaled, debris additive, `footprintRadius` on
the wire from either producer, the `fpToPx` call dropped, and the budget folded into the reach.

**What is NOT verified, stated so it is not mistaken for covered.** Every measurement above is
in world px, taken off the particle system's own state; **no frame was sampled.** How the ring
reads against a dissolving silhouette at real camera zoom is still a looking-question, and this
project's own record on that is bad enough to be worth the sentence (see volume 18's 0.01-luma
door). And `DEBRIS_REACH_R` = 1.6 and the 0.8 lift are *values, not properties*: the tests
assert that the code derives them from the body, and deliberately not that 1.6 is the right
number. A test pinned to a shipped tuning constant fails the next time anyone tunes it.
