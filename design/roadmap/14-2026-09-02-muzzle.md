## The gun steps onto the line its own bullets fly along (2026-09-02, client only)

*"你看看最后的那几次射击，没有怪物的情况下，明显能看到子弹从枪口出去后进行了弧形的漂移，然后才直线运动。"*
Handed over with a replay file. The third report in a row about this one correction — after
[the gun never moved](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#the-gun-never-moved-and-the-flash-was-aimed-at-the-floor-2026-08-30-client--engine)
(08-30, the fx were being burst at the sim's muzzle point) and
[a slow bullet's curve](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#a-slow-bullets-curve-budgeted-by-distance-instead-of-a-clock-2026-08-30-client)
(08-30, the ease budget was a clock instead of a distance) — and the first one to be about
the shape of the path rather than about where it starts or how long it takes.

### What the recorded run said

The file replayed at `ENGINE_VERSION` 50 against a 51 engine, so the one sim file the bump
had touched was checked out at the older commit for the measurement (`DoorSystem.ts`; `git
show --stat` first, so this took minutes rather than a whole worktree). 63 player shots, and
the last two — ticks 1927 and 2357, the tick the recorder was fired at — were a `blaster`
aimed **straight up the screen with no enemies alive**, exactly the described scene.

Fed through the shipped `Bullet` and the shipped muzzle chain, frame by frame:

```
D = (-20.8, +0.9) world px  →  20.8 px PERPENDICULAR to the shot
f2: 43° off-aim   f3: 39°   f4: 34°   f5: 29°
f6: 23°           f7: 16°   f8: 8°    f9: 1°   → 0° thereafter
```

The round left the barrel **43° off its own aim** and slid 20.8 world px sideways over nine
render frames (~150 ms, ~43 px of travel) before flying straight. At the room camera's
~3.4–4.5x that is **~79 screen px of drift**. Not an illusion, and not a tuning matter.

### Why a correction across the shot can only be a curve

`Bullet.setMuzzleOrigin` draws a round at the shooter's real barrel tip and eases the offset
onto the sim's authoritative position. Split that offset into its two components and they
have completely different visual consequences:

- **along the shot** — the round appears to travel a little fast or a little slow. Invisible.
- **across the shot** — the only way to spend it is to move the drawn round sideways *while
  it flies forward*. That is an arc, at whatever rate it is eased. Easing it more gently
  spreads the same drift over more of the flight; it cannot remove it.

So the fix is not in the ease. Both sources of a cross-track offset were removed where they
arose, and the ease is left with a pure along-track distance:

- **Sideways.** The active weapon socket sat at its **rest angle** and only the module *spun*
  to the aim, so the bone tip it hangs off was a fixed body-local point — 52 authoring px out
  on the hero's right. Fire straight up and the drawn gun is a socket-length off the line its
  bullets fly along. `rigWeaponMount.orbitActiveSocketToAim` now turns the **bone** to the aim
  before FK runs, i.e. it actually orbits, which is what `13`'s *"two weapon modules that
  orbit it"* described all along. The enemy `held` mount lost its 0.45 vertical squash at the
  same time and for the same reason: unsquashed, in the ground plane, is not a taste call —
  `Entity.applyTransform` draws the ground 1:1 (`screen.y = groundY - z`), so anything else
  puts a drawn gun off its own bullets' line.
- **Height.** `bulletZ` is a gameplay band (0.5 grid — *"chest height, clears ground-hug
  hazards, blocked by tall cover"*, `07`) while the gun is drawn wherever the art hangs it:
  16 vs 26.4 world px for the hero. That gap points straight up the screen, so it is
  perpendicular to every horizontal shot. `muzzlePos()` now reports its own height alongside
  its point — they were always asked together — and `Bullet.setDrawnHeight` draws the round at
  the gun's height. Render-only: `z` is untouched and the sim never sees it.

What is left is the blaster's +9.5 px of extra reach, along the shot, which is what
`muzzleParity`'s table has measured since 08-30. `setMuzzleOrigin` also **projects** whatever
it is handed onto the shot direction now, so *"a bullet is never bent"* is a property of the
renderer rather than an outcome of how well two content tables happen to agree.

### The regression that 4155 tests could not see, and one frame could

The first version of the sideways half orbited the **module** instead of the bone. It passed
everything: the full client suite, `tsc`, the file-length gate, a new 24-angle geometry sweep,
a four-mutation battery. Then one live frame:

| | ring (`socket_r` art) | weapon module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aiming south | (52, −49.7) | (0, 6) |

**71 px apart**, with the energy tether still drawn out to where the ring was. The ring, the
tether and the module's contact shade on the core all hang off that bone; moving only the
module left the gun floating free of its own mount. Turning the bone instead carries all four
for free and let `activeModuleMount` go back to simply reading the bone tip.

The reason no test saw it generalises past this rig: **every check asserted where the module
IS** — mount coordinates, muzzle point, cross-track component, depth flip — **and none
asserted that it is still ATTACHED to the thing that holds it**, because a rig's parts have
that relationship by construction, right up until one of them is moved out of the FK chain,
which is the moment the construction stops being the guarantee.

### The test gap, in two layers

**`muzzleParity.test.ts` measured a rotating chain at exactly one pose.** The file's own
header said so plainly — *"Everything is evaluated at aim = 0, the one pose where the whole
chain is a single scalar reach along the aim ray"* — and that sentence reads as a
simplification when it is the blind spot: at aim 0 the disagreement is almost entirely
along-track, and the component that draws an arc is nearly zero for reasons that have nothing
to do with the art being right. It now sweeps **24 aim angles × every ranged weapon × every
carrying body**, bounds the perpendicular component at 0.1 world px, carries a control that
fires the same measurement on the old geometry (20.8 px), and flies a real `Bullet` through
the reported shot end to end (0° off-aim where it measured 43°).

**`rigComposition.test.ts` gained the attachment invariants** — relationships between things
the renderer positions *independently*, over the real shipped bundles, swept across 16 aim
directions and every armed body:

- the module and its own socket ring are the same point (two independent placement paths);
- and stay so through every shipped clip, so the gun rides the hover bob with its ring;
- the drawn tether's own far endpoint reaches the module, read out of the signature
  `drawTethers` builds from the curve it actually strokes rather than recomputed;
- a held (mob) gun sits at the **same** distance from its body in every direction — the
  invariant the 0.45 squash broke;
- plus the anti-vacuity control: the module has to actually travel with the aim, or every
  "stays attached" check above passes for free.

### Two things the sweeps turned up on their own

- **The socket mount never picked up its own bone's clip translate**, so the ring rode the
  idle bob and the module hanging on it did not — 6 authoring px at the extreme, 0.9 px on the
  frame where it was first noticed. Predates this pass and is the same defect in miniature;
  fixing it is what lets the clip sweep assert the two coincide *exactly* rather than merely
  overlapping.
- **`rigComposition`'s own harness built its `RigSkin` without the `bodyFill` the game always
  passes** (`Skin.ts`: `new RigSkin(loaded.rig, loaded.bundle, loaded.bodyFill)`), so
  critter-core's held gun mounted 15 authoring px further out in the harness than in the game.
  A harness quietly less faithful than the thing it tests.
