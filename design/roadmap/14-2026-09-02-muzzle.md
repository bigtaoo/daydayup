## The gun steps onto the line its own bullets fly along (2026-09-02, client only)

*"你看看最后的那几次射击，没有怪物的情况下，明显能看到子弹从枪口出去后进行了弧形的漂移，然后才直线运动。"*
Handed over with a replay file. The third report in a row about this one correction — after
[the gun never moved](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#the-gun-never-moved-and-the-flash-was-aimed-at-the-floor-2026-08-30-client--engine)
(08-30, the fx were being burst at the sim's muzzle point) and
[a slow bullet's curve](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#a-slow-bullets-curve-budgeted-by-distance-instead-of-a-clock-2026-08-30-client)
(08-30, the ease budget was a clock instead of a distance) — and the first one to be about
the shape of the path rather than about where it starts or how long it takes.

### What the recorded run said

The file replayed at `ENGINE_VERSION` 50 against a 51 engine, so the one sim file the bump
had touched was checked out at the older commit for the measurement (`DoorSystem.ts`; `git
show --stat` first, so this took minutes rather than a whole worktree). 63 player shots, and
the last two — ticks 1927 and 2357, the tick the recorder was fired at — were a `blaster`
aimed **straight up the screen with no enemies alive**, exactly the described scene.

Fed through the shipped `Bullet` and the shipped muzzle chain, frame by frame:

```
D = (-20.8, +0.9) world px  →  20.8 px PERPENDICULAR to the shot
f2: 43° off-aim   f3: 39°   f4: 34°   f5: 29°
f6: 23°           f7: 16°   f8: 8°    f9: 1°   → 0° thereafter
```

The round left the barrel **43° off its own aim** and slid 20.8 world px sideways over nine
render frames (~150 ms, ~43 px of travel) before flying straight. At the room camera's
~3.4–4.5x that is **~79 screen px of drift**. Not an illusion, and not a tuning matter.

### Why a correction across the shot can only be a curve

`Bullet.setMuzzleOrigin` draws a round at the shooter's real barrel tip and eases the offset
onto the sim's authoritative position. Split that offset into its two components and they
have completely different visual consequences:

- **along the shot** — the round appears to travel a little fast or a little slow. Invisible.
- **across the shot** — the only way to spend it is to move the drawn round sideways *while
  it flies forward*. That is an arc, at whatever rate it is eased. Easing it more gently
  spreads the same drift over more of the flight; it cannot remove it.

So the fix is not in the ease. Both sources of a cross-track offset were removed where they
arose, and the ease is left with a pure along-track distance:

- **Sideways.** The active weapon socket sat at its **rest angle** and only the module *spun*
  to the aim, so the bone tip it hangs off was a fixed body-local point — 52 authoring px out
  on the hero's right. Fire straight up and the drawn gun is a socket-length off the line its
  bullets fly along. `rigWeaponMount.orbitActiveSocketToAim` now turns the **bone** to the aim
  before FK runs, i.e. it actually orbits, which is what `13`'s *"two weapon modules that
  orbit it"* described all along. The enemy `held` mount lost its 0.45 vertical squash at the
  same time and for the same reason: unsquashed, in the ground plane, is not a taste call —
  `Entity.applyTransform` draws the ground 1:1 (`screen.y = groundY - z`), so anything else
  puts a drawn gun off its own bullets' line.
- **Height.** `bulletZ` is a gameplay band (0.5 grid — *"chest height, clears ground-hug
  hazards, blocked by tall cover"*, `07`) while the gun is drawn wherever the art hangs it:
  16 vs 26.4 world px for the hero. That gap points straight up the screen, so it is
  perpendicular to every horizontal shot. `muzzlePos()` now reports its own height alongside
  its point — they were always asked together — and `Bullet.setDrawnHeight` draws the round at
  the gun's height. Render-only: `z` is untouched and the sim never sees it.

What is left is the blaster's +9.5 px of extra reach, along the shot, which is what
`muzzleParity`'s table has measured since 08-30. `setMuzzleOrigin` also **projects** whatever
it is handed onto the shot direction now, so *"a bullet is never bent"* is a property of the
renderer rather than an outcome of how well two content tables happen to agree.

### The regression that 4155 tests could not see, and one frame could

The first version of the sideways half orbited the **module** instead of the bone. It passed
everything: the full client suite, `tsc`, the file-length gate, a new 24-angle geometry sweep,
a four-mutation battery. Then one live frame:

| | ring (`socket_r` art) | weapon module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aiming south | (52, −49.7) | (0, 6) |

**71 px apart**, with the energy tether still drawn out to where the ring was. The ring, the
tether and the module's contact shade on the core all hang off that bone; moving only the
module left the gun floating free of its own mount. Turning the bone instead carries all four
for free and let `activeModuleMount` go back to simply reading the bone tip.

The reason no test saw it generalises past this rig: **every check asserted where the module
IS** — mount coordinates, muzzle point, cross-track component, depth flip — **and none
asserted that it is still ATTACHED to the thing that holds it**, because a rig's parts have
that relationship by construction, right up until one of them is moved out of the FK chain,
which is the moment the construction stops being the guarantee.

### The test gap, in two layers

**`muzzleParity.test.ts` measured a rotating chain at exactly one pose.** The file's own
header said so plainly — *"Everything is evaluated at aim = 0, the one pose where the whole
chain is a single scalar reach along the aim ray"* — and that sentence reads as a
simplification when it is the blind spot: at aim 0 the disagreement is almost entirely
along-track, and the component that draws an arc is nearly zero for reasons that have nothing
to do with the art being right. It now sweeps **24 aim angles × every ranged weapon × every
carrying body**, bounds the perpendicular component at 0.1 world px, carries a control that
fires the same measurement on the old geometry (20.8 px), and flies a real `Bullet` through
the reported shot end to end (0° off-aim where it measured 43°).

**`rigComposition.test.ts` gained the attachment invariants** — relationships between things
the renderer positions *independently*, over the real shipped bundles, swept across 16 aim
directions and every armed body:

- the module and its own socket ring are the same point (two independent placement paths);
- and stay so through every shipped clip, so the gun rides the hover bob with its ring;
- the drawn tether's own far endpoint reaches the module, read out of the signature
  `drawTethers` builds from the curve it actually strokes rather than recomputed;
- a held (mob) gun sits at the **same** distance from its body in every direction — the
  invariant the 0.45 squash broke;
- plus the anti-vacuity control: the module has to actually travel with the aim, or every
  "stays attached" check above passes for free.

### Two things the sweeps turned up on their own

- **The socket mount never picked up its own bone's clip translate**, so the ring rode the
  idle bob and the module hanging on it did not — 6 authoring px at the extreme, 0.9 px on the
  frame where it was first noticed. Predates this pass and is the same defect in miniature;
  fixing it is what lets the clip sweep assert the two coincide *exactly* rather than merely
  overlapping.
- **`rigComposition`'s own harness built its `RigSkin` without the `bodyFill` the game always
  passes** (`Skin.ts`: `new RigSkin(loaded.rig, loaded.bundle, loaded.bodyFill)`), so
  critter-core's held gun mounted 15 authoring px further out in the harness than in the game.
  A harness quietly less faithful than the thing it tests.

## The character gets an attack, and the sim finally says when (2026-09-02, engine + client + art, ENGINE_VERSION 51→52)

A question rather than a report — *"角色现在有攻击动画吗？射击的和拿刀时的"* — and the honest
answer was **half a shooting one, and nothing at all for a blade**. A shot played a procedural
recoil envelope (2026-08-30). A swing played nothing, and could not have: **the sim never told
the render layer a swing happened.** `deflect` fires only when a swing catches a bullet and `hit`
only when it connects, so a sword swung at empty air produced no event of any kind.

Three things were missing, and they are three different kinds of missing.

**1. The enemy bundles had no clips to play.** `boss-core`, `brute-core`, `critter-core` and
`floater-core` shipped `idle`/`hurt`/`death`/`spawn` — no `move`, no `attack`. The missing `move`
was a live bug of its own, not just a gap: `Actor` has asked for `'move'` while an actor is moving
since the rig runtime landed, and `RigSkin.playClip` resolves an unknown name to **no clip at
all** — so a walking mob fell through to a bare rest pose and lost even its idle bob. All four
gained both clips, authored to the same shape the hero bundles use (a 0.6 s two-hop gait; a 0.35 s
wind-up → lunge → settle), plus the boss's own version of each in its shard rings, since its
weapon mount mode is `'none'` and the rings are its armament.

**2. The `attack` clips could not be played, and that was structural.** All three `char_*` bundles
had carried one since the rig landed and it had never been played once. Clips are sampled WHOLE,
and `playClip` swapped `this.clip` outright — so playing `attack` dropped every bone it does not
track back to rest for its whole duration. `render/rigClipLayer.ts` is the additive layer the
2026-08-30 note predicted would be needed: `rotation`/`translate` ADD, `scale`/`alpha` MULTIPLY,
and a bone the overlay never names comes through untouched. The base clip keeps running
underneath, so `socket_l` keeps its hover bob through a shot and a `move` cycle keeps walking
through an attack.

That buys one authoring rule, and it is a claim about DATA that no correct code can rescue: **an
attack clip must start and end at identity**, or the layer steps the pose on the frame it triggers
and again on the frame it expires. `rigComposition.test.ts` asserts it per bundle, along with the
full six-clip vocabulary, that every attack bone exists on its own rig, and that every looping clip
returns to its own first pose.

**3. A blade cannot be swung by a clip, for two independent reasons.** A clip's `translateX` is
applied in RIG space (`sprite.x = pose.ex + transform.translateX`), so an authored `-10` slides the
gun *left*, not backwards along its own barrel — which is exactly what the hero's original `attack`
clip did, at exactly `RECOIL_MODULE_PX`'s magnitude, so the two would have doubled up; that
translate was dropped from the clip and the assertion that it stays gone is in the composition
suite. And the weapon sockets are **aim-tracking bones**: `RigSkin` overwrites their rotation with
the aim angle every frame, so an authored swing arc is discarded in silence. `rigRecoil.ts` became
`rigAttackMotion.ts` and grew the melee half — a three-phase arc (cock 22° behind the aim, sweep to
46° ahead, recover to exactly the aim line) plus a forward lunge, against the gun's backward kick
and lean. `bodyPx` is one SIGNED number for both kinds so the renderer keeps one formula.

**The engine change, and why it is an event.** `WeaponFireSystem`'s melee branch now emits
`melee_swing`, with the identical field list to `bullet_fired` so the render layer answers both
with one reaction (`Actor.onAttack(kind)`). It could have been a render-side read of
`weapon.justSwung`, which is already hashed sim state — and that would have been wrong: it is a
**one-tick latch**, and `GameLoop.advanceOnline` drains every confirmed frame the server has ready
and then reconciles the scene ONCE, against the last of them. Every swing on an intermediate frame
would be invisible, at a rate set by latency. `session.drive()` returns the whole drained batch.

**The bump is measured, not assumed, and the measurement only works before it.** This engine
serializes `version: ENGINE_VERSION` into the hashed state, so bumping alone moves all five golden
hashes and a post-bump run cannot tell "the event changed the sim" from "the version changed". Run
against the **v51** fixture with the emit in place: all five hashes still matched, and the only
failures were the five `witness` assertions, each gaining one key — `melee_swing` at 7 / 27 / 36 /
44 / 67 across the scenarios. So nothing diverges; the bump is what `versionContract.test.ts`
demands before the witness re-record is accepted, and it is still right, because a v51 and a v52
client disagree about what a stream *emits*. Those counts are also the first evidence the golden
set exercises the melee path at all.

**Two files went back under the 500-line convention rather than into the baseline.**
`canonicalAimRad` — the pre-mirror space every body-space offset must be stated in — moved from a
`RigSkin` private to `facing.ts`, where the mirror rule already lives, and got its own tests for
the first time (four call sites depend on it and none of them could reach it before). The
module-contact repaint moved to `rigShading.ts` beside the painter it delegates to, form ①. Net:
`RigSkin.ts` 500 → 481.

**Testing: 86 new cases, and 35 mutants across two batteries.** Client 4221 / engine 1104, both
green.

The first battery — 15 mutants over the attack path — matters more for how it ran than for what it
found. Its first attempt **crashed inside the test runner after applying mutant #1 and before
reverting it**, leaving a live `scaleX` override in the source; every row printed after that was
measured against a tree that was already failing, and every row still said KILLED. That is the
whole argument for baselining first and reverting in a `finally`. Re-run properly (baseline 4221
passed / 0 failed), all 15 died, including the two visible only as *shape*: the melee lunge taking
the recoil's sign (a character recoiling from their own sword), and the eye following the blade
instead of the target.

The second battery, 20 mutants over the coverage pass, produced **three survivors, and two of them
were real weaknesses in tests written minutes earlier**:

- *the body-relative mount check.* It bounded the drawn x (`< 30` for a mount 30 px out), and
  `drawModuleContacts` pulls each ellipse so far in from its mount that a rig-space 40 still
  cleared the bar. Restated as an invariance — same relative geometry at a different absolute
  position must draw identically — it kills.
- *the world-px readability check.* A single `or` across three channels, so scaling every enemy
  translation to 5% (a 2.72 wp lunge reduced to 0.14 wp, invisible) went on passing on the 14%
  squash alone. The `or` stays, because boss-core genuinely expresses its attack without
  translating; a second assertion now says a clip that translates AT ALL must keep it above the bar.
- The third is a true equivalent, recorded rather than dressed up: `AttackMotion.advance`'s
  `Math.max(0, …)` clamp is unobservable, because `progress` gates on `ms <= 0`, which a negative
  `ms` satisfies exactly as zero does, and nothing accumulates across a `kick()`.

**Eight of that second battery are DATA mutants — edits to the shipped `animation.json` files,
which no source-level test can reach.** All eight die: an attack clip that stops ending at
identity, a loop whose seam stops closing, `brute-core` drifting from the `critter-core` it is a
hand-maintained copy of, one bundle's attack changing duration, a clip naming a bone its rig does
not have, a lunge authored past its own silhouette, and the readability row above. That is the
half of this pass no fake-bundle test could ever cover, and it is the half where the art actually
lives.

**Also found and fixed while checking that the new clips were reproducible:**
`tools/animator/scripts/build-critter-project.mjs` had drifted from the bundle it writes in two
ways at once — it re-emitted `body.png` from the raw 1254 px source over the shipped 256 px
downsample (52 KB → 1 MB in the run pack), and re-derived the binding scale against that raw
source, shrinking the critter to a fifth of its drawn size. Both were caught immediately by
`rigComposition.test.ts` once the regenerated file was used, which is the only reason this is a
footnote instead of a shipped regression. The script now treats the shipped bundle as ground truth
for both — the same conclusion `build-character-projects.mjs` had already reached about its own
`orbWorldWidth`, and that script now sources its `animations` from the shipped bundle too, so a
re-run can no longer silently revert clips authored against it.

**Still open, deliberately.** `hurt`/`death`/`spawn` remain authored-and-unplayed in every bundle:
they need GameState signals `Actor` does not receive yet, which is a different piece of wiring from
this one. A swing has no audio cue of its own — `deflect` covers the parry and there is no whoosh
in the catalogue. And the idle↔move base-clip swap still has no cross-fade, so `move`'s constant
10° shell lean pops on the frame a character starts or stops walking; the new enemy `move` clips
are authored to start and end at identity specifically so they do not make that worse.
